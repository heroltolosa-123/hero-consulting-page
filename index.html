Why a statistician, not a formatter

Most dissertation help stops at grammar and formatting. My work starts where that ends — at whether your sampling design holds up, whether your model specification matches your hypotheses, and whether your results section will survive a panel that knows what to ask. I know what that panel is looking for, because I've sat on that side of the table too, evaluating research defenses at PUP.

I've built credit risk models at a listed bank, forecasting models at Accenture, and taught graduate statistics for six years at PUP. That range is what lets me sit across from a physician doing an IPA study one week, and an executive running a mixed-methods dissertation the next — and speak both languages: the discipline's and the data's.

Clients are typically physicians, senior executives, and working professionals completing a Master's or Doctorate while holding down a full-time career. The engagement is built around that reality.

Questions Before You Start

The things people ask on the diagnostic call, answered here so you can decide whether to book one.

I'm already halfway through. Is it too late to bring in a statistician?

No. Most engagements begin mid-stream, usually right after a panel returns comments on the methodology or the results chapter. Coming in later simply changes the scope: the first step becomes assessing what holds and what has to be reworked, rather than designing from scratch.

My panel rejected my statistical treatment. Can that be fixed?

Usually, yes. Rejected treatments generally trace back to one of a few causes: a test that does not match the level of measurement, a design that cannot answer the stated research questions, or assumptions that were never checked. The work is to identify which of those applies, correct it, and prepare you to explain the change to the same panel that raised it.

Do you work with qualitative studies, or only numbers?

Both. Qualitative work (interpretative phenomenological analysis, phenomenology, thematic coding) is a regular part of the practice, as is mixed-methods design where the two strands have to be integrated rather than simply reported side by side.

Which software will my analysis be done in?

SPSS, R, STATA, or Python, whichever your institution expects or you are most comfortable defending. If your school requires SPSS output in a particular format, that is what you receive. Tell me the requirement at the diagnostic call.

How long does it take?

It depends entirely on scope and on the state of your data. A sample size computation is a short piece of work; a full analysis chapter on a dataset that still needs cleaning is not. Rather than quote a generic turnaround, the scoped proposal in Stage B carries a timeline tied to your specific deliverables, and it is built around your defense date, so bring that date to the call.

Do you follow my university's format?

Yes. Tables, statistical notation, and chapter structure are produced to your institution's manual, APA or otherwise. Send the template or a recently approved manuscript from your program and deliverables are matched to it.

Is this considered cheating?

No, and this is worth being precise about. Consulting a statistician on research design and analysis is standard practice in academic and clinical research; it is why published studies routinely acknowledge one. What distinguishes it from misconduct is that the work is disclosed and that you can account for every decision in it. That is the standard this practice holds to, which is why the process includes walking you through the reasoning rather than only handing over output. See Ethics & Confidentiality above.

I'm outside Metro Manila, or abroad. Does that work?

Yes. Sessions run by video call or asynchronously through email and messaging, which is how most engagements with working professionals run regardless of location. In-person sessions are possible where practical.

What does the free diagnostic call actually cover?

A review of your topic, your current chapter status, and any feedback your panel has already given. That is enough to tell you what the work would involve and whether it is worth engaging at all. There is no cost and no obligation to proceed.
