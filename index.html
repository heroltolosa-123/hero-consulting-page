FAQ

Questions before you start.

What types of research projects do you support?

Undergraduate theses, master's research, doctoral dissertations, journal manuscripts, institutional and organizational research, and applied analytics work. Quantitative, qualitative and mixed-methods designs are all in scope.

Can you help determine the appropriate statistical method?

Yes, and this is usually where engagements begin. The right test follows from your research questions, your design, and your level of measurement — not from what is most familiar. Part of the work is being able to justify that choice to a panel or reviewer.

Do you assist with sample size and power analysis?

Yes. You receive a defensible sample size computation with the assumptions stated and cited, written so it can go straight into your methodology chapter.

Can you interpret and explain statistical results?

Yes. Output on its own is not a finding. The work includes translating results into plain language, stating the limitations honestly, and preparing you to answer questions about them in your own words.

Do you work with existing datasets?

Yes. Bring data you have already collected, secondary data, or administrative records. The first step is assessing what the data can actually support before any analysis is run.

Can you help with R, Python, SPSS, or other statistical software?

Yes — R, Python, SPSS and Stata, plus SQL for data preparation. If your institution requires a particular package or output format, that is what you receive.

How do you determine project scope and pricing?

Scope comes out of an initial conversation about your objectives and the state of your data. Pricing is then a fixed fee in Philippine pesos for a defined deliverable, not an hourly rate, so the figure does not move because a stage took longer than expected.

How can I start a consultation?

Send the inquiry form on this page, or email or message directly. Include your topic, where you are in the process, and any feedback you have already received — that is enough to scope a first conversation.
